The builder track carries the measured numbers, the three named configurations (economy / proof / peak observation), and a new hardening lesson: three operator-quality gaps — hollow turns, search counted as work, content amnesia — were caught in fast-forward simulation before going live. Simulate the week before you live it.
- **The harness, named — eleven components** (Builder chs 5, 11, 14): the reference platform mapped its own harness component by component, and the map is now in the book as something to measure your own system against — loop, skill selection, context assembly, memory, policy, verification, self-correction, escalation, learning, observability, resumption. With it come three rulings that generalize: the harness is a platform primitive (not a feature of the agent module), it is model-agnostic by construction (swapping engines changes the engine, not the chassis), and **every incident becomes a harness component, permanently** — the harness hardens with age instead of decaying. Chapter 11 gains the Trace, the read model that turns "why did the agent do that?" from hand-written SQL into a surface — and makes *"audit the agent the way you audit an employee"* real rather than a slogan. Chapter 14 gains the resumption rule a proof run bought: a paused chain resumes only where every completed step is provably idempotent; where the guarantees end, a human belongs.
- **The harness bridge** (Builder ch 5 + both glossaries): through 2026 the industry settled on a word for everything this book calls the control plane — the *harness*. Chapter 5 now makes the translation explicit: Anthropic calls its own Agent SDK "a general-purpose agent harness," the March 2026 Claude Code source leak showed the world 513,000 lines of exactly this scaffolding, and the Meta-Harness paper gave it numbers. The book was betting on the harness before the word arrived; the industry naming it is the thesis being accepted. Both glossaries carry the term.
- **"The Two Tiers of Tool Metadata"** (Builder ch 15, with pointers from chs 9 and 19): what the agent sees when it *decides* is not what it sees when it *acts* — lazily-loaded instructions arrive after the choice is made, so a behavioral rule that lives only there is invisible exactly when it should change the decision. The rule: decides → description; executes → instructions. Proven by a fully logged three-round production case (the 24-second guessed blog post, the instructions-fix that didn't bite, and the log-verified fix: moving one sentence made the agent search first and cite its source — nothing else changed). The section shipped only after round three verified the ending; a chapter about verify-don't-trust does not get an unverified conclusion.
- **The dead-weight cut** (Builder Edition, 25 chapters + appendix B): a full editorial audit asked one question of every section — *does this drive the technical reader's build forward?* — and cut ~10,000 words (12 percent of the track) that didn't. What went: market-watching (star counts, valuations, version changelogs, ecosystem tours), a 1,700-word speculative procurement narrative (now a 400-word declared `hypothesis` built on shipped primitives), twice-taught security defenses, triple-told mechanisms, and rotting price tables. What stayed: every logged proof, every war story, all the unglamorous internals — the audit confirmed the moat chapters were already tight. Appendix B was rewritten from a tool feature-tour into what it should have been: the story of how this book is itself produced agentically, with the author as editor-in-chief and the git history as audit trail. The foreword gained a core path ("these ten chapters are the machine"), and the editorial checklist gained a standing rot guard: a market signal gets one home, an as-of date, and must change what the reader builds — or it goes in a post, not the book.
- **"Two Tools, Five Hundred Skills"** (Builder chs 12, 15, 17, 19): the MCP surface's answer to client-side tool bloat, now documented — full list, group filtering (`?groups=sales`), or the dispatch pair: `search_skills` + `execute_skill`, two meta-tools that stand in for the entire catalog. One scorer, two consumers, on both sides of the wire.

The business track's audit found one true offender ("silence has no webhook", now a declared refrain).
